The active commuter's dilemma

Cycling or running to work is great until a squall hits halfway, a crosswind on the river path pushes you around, or an icy patch appears on a shaded corner. The usual fix is to check the forecast every evening, but you need very specific answers: will it rain between 7:30 and 8:15, will gusts be above my comfort level, will the path freeze overnight?

Weather Alerts, an Android app, answers those questions for you. You save your home and workplace, create alerts for your personal limits and choose the timing. The Weather Alerts site shows how the app watches the conditions you pick and sends a notification when they match.

Commute rules worth creating

Here are the conditions most active commuters find useful. Thresholds are entirely up to you.

Morning rain

A rain start alert with a moderate or heavy threshold for your home location.

Headwinds and crosswinds

Wind speed and gust limits for both ends of the route; exposed bridges and riverside paths feel gusts most.

Ice risk

Freezing-conditions alerts the evening before, so you can fit winter tyres, take the shoes with grip or choose the gritted route.

Hot ride home

Heat and heat index alerts for the afternoon commute, a reminder to carry extra water.

Low visibility

Visibility and fog alerts for dark winter mornings, a prompt to check your lights.

City air

AQI and PM2.5 thresholds, helpful on smoggy days when you might prefer a quieter route or public transport.

How to configure it

  1. Save start and finish. Add home and work, and maybe the gym or a client's office you ride to.
  2. Set limits and timing. Choose the threshold for each condition and when to hear about it: immediately, a few hours ahead or up to seven days before.
  3. Check, then go. When an alert arrives, look at the hourly forecast and radar to see the timing, then decide on kit, route or mode.

Following the weather as you move

Route weather helps you monitor rain, snow, wind, storms, visibility and temperature along a journey, and live location weather keeps forecasts updated as your location changes. For long commutes across town, that means the forecast you see reflects where you actually are.

The live radar shows rain and storms moving across the map; a quick look before setting off can reveal whether a shower will pass in ten minutes. The lightning tracker helps on summer evenings when thunderstorms build after work.

Kit decisions made easy

Rain alert fired

Mudguards on, waterproof jacket and overshoes, spare socks in the pannier or running pack.

Gust alert fired

Lower-profile route, avoid the exposed bridge, leave a few minutes earlier.

Freezing alert fired

Gloves, grippy tyres or shoes, and a route that is likely to be gritted.

Heat alert fired

Lighter layers, extra water, and maybe a shower at work planned in.

Over time your alerts become a quiet routine: the evening notification tells you what to pack, and the morning is just about getting out of the door.

Fair-weather days are worth knowing about too

Alerts are not only for bad news. Some commuters set rules for pleasant conditions as well, for example temperature rising above a comfortable level with no rain forecast, so they know which days are perfect for a longer route home or a run commute instead of the bus.

A week of active commuting

Sunday night: a heavy-rain alert for Tuesday morning lands, so you plan Tuesday as a work-from-home day. Wednesday evening: a gust alert for the river path on Thursday afternoon means you take the sheltered back streets home. Friday dawn: a freezing-conditions alert from the night before reminded you to swap to studded tyres, and the slippery bridge is a non-event.

Riding in the dark months

Winter commuting brings the hardest conditions: early darkness, wet leaves, ice on bridges and gusty fronts rolling in from the west. A combination of rules covers most of it. Use a freezing-conditions alert the evening before, a visibility alert for foggy mornings, a heavy-rain alert for the morning slot and a gust rule for exposed stretches. With those in place, the dark months feel far more predictable, and you can enjoy the clear, crisp days when they come.

Keep alerts timed to your routine

Active Hours let routine notifications arrive in the evening when you plan the next day, and Quiet Hours keep them silent while you sleep, while critical warnings can still get through. Custom sounds and home-screen widgets keep the routine fast: one glance at the widget shows current conditions and any active alerts.

Data and Premium

Frequently asked questions

Can I get an alert if it will rain during my ride to work?

Yes. Set a rain threshold for your home or work location and choose when to be notified.

Can I set a wind limit for cycling?

Yes. Wind speed and gust thresholds can be set separately.

Will I be warned about icy mornings?

A freezing-conditions or low-temperature alert can notify you the evening before.

Is there an air quality alert for running in the city?

You can set AQI and PM2.5 thresholds for your saved locations.

Does it update while I am riding?

Live location weather updates forecasts as your location changes, and route weather tracks conditions along the way.

Can I check if a shower will pass before I leave?

The live radar shows rain moving across the map, so you can judge the timing.

Does it work for walking commutes too?

Yes. Any commuter can set rain, ice, heat or wind alerts for their route.
